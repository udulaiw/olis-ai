# Climate of Sri Lanka

Sri Lanka's rainfall comes mainly from the monsoon winds and the inter-monsoon periods between them.

## Monsoon seasons

| Season | Months | Main rainfall |
|---|---|---|
| First inter-monsoon | March – April | Convectional rain, thunderstorms, mostly in the south-west |
| South-west monsoon | May – September | Heavy rain on the south-western slopes and the wet zone |
| Second inter-monsoon | October – November | Rain over most of the island; cyclones from the Bay of Bengal are possible |
| North-east monsoon | December – February | Rain mainly in the north, east and the dry zone |

The central highlands block the monsoon winds: the windward side gets heavy orographic rain, the leeward side stays drier (a rain shadow).

## Climatic zones

- **Wet zone**: south-west of the island and the western slopes of the hills; annual rainfall above about 2500 mm.
- **Intermediate zone**: between the wet and dry zones; about 1750–2500 mm.
- **Dry zone**: north, north-central, east and south-east; below about 1750 mm, mostly from the north-east monsoon.

## Physical facts often asked

- Highest mountain: Pidurutalagala (about 2524 m).
- Longest river: Mahaweli Ganga (about 335 km).
